Why there is no radical expression

The primitive integer polynomial stays degree 14 modulo each of the following primes, and its factors are squarefree. The exact factor degrees are:

PrimeIrreducible factor degrees
5914
831, 2, 11
1511, 13

Irreducibility modulo 59 proves irreducibility over the rationals, hence the Galois group acts transitively on the 14 roots. The Dedekind–Frobenius theorem gives a 13-cycle from the factorization modulo 151. Any nontrivial block system would have blocks of size 2 or 7. The 13-cycle must fix every block and then every point inside it, which is impossible. Thus the action is primitive.

The factorization modulo 83 gives a permutation with disjoint cycles of lengths 1, 2 and 11. Its eleventh power is a transposition. A primitive group containing a transposition is the full symmetric group: the pairs swapped by its conjugates form a graph whose connected components are invariant blocks; primitivity makes the graph connected, and the corresponding edge transpositions generate all permutations. Consequently the Galois group is , which is not solvable. By Galois’s radical-solvability criterion, no root of this irreducible polynomial has an expression by radicals over the rationals.

Why an algebraic root rather than simple radicals?

For N = 11, the defining degree-11 polynomial has Galois group S₁₁. Consequently, this representative's coefficients cannot all be expressed by radicals over the rationals. The isolated-root form is an exact analytical description.

Exact Galois-group check

The polynomial is irreducible over the rationals, so its Galois group is transitive of prime degree 11 and therefore primitive. Modulo 71 it is squarefree with irreducible factor degrees 2 and 9. By the Dedekind–Frobenius theorem, the corresponding permutation has cycle type (2)(9), whose ninth power is a transposition. A primitive permutation group containing a transposition is the full symmetric group. Finally, , so radical expressions for all four coefficients would give a radical expression for B. The verifier checks irreducibility and the modular factorization exactly.

Here is the elementary group step: conjugate the transposition by the group and regard the resulting swapped pairs as edges. Connected components form an invariant block system. Primitivity forces the graph to be connected, and its edge transpositions generate the full symmetric group.
