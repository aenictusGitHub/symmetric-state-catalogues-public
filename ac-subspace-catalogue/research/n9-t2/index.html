Nine qubits: second-order maximum dimension is one

A two-dimensional second-order anticoherent subspace is impossible. The proof below uses the full cross conditions between basis vectors and an exact positive certificate. It applies to arbitrary complex subspaces.

PROVED MAXIMUM · PAPER CONJECTURE CONFIRMEDk_{\max}(9,2)=1

An explicit pure state gives the lower bound. The four-copy argument excludes every subspace of dimension two or larger.

Whole-subspace cross conditions→ antisymmetric basis vector→ positive four-copy moment→ exact contradiction

An exact state attains dimension one

|\psi\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{13}\,|D_9^{(0)}\rangle+\sqrt{30}\,|D_9^{(4)}\rangle-\sqrt{21}\,|D_9^{(8)}\rangle}{8}

Its two-qubit reduced state is exactly \rho_2=I_{\mathrm{Sym}^2}/3. Thus its span is a one-dimensional second-order AC subspace. This is an existing catalogue witness, not a new state construction.

1. Keep the cross conditions

Suppose an orthonormal pair u,v\in V_{9/2} spans a second-order AC subspace, and write P=|u\rangle\langle u|+|v\rangle\langle v|. For every spin tensor of rank one or two, the whole-subspace condition is

P T_{Lq}P=0,\qquad L=1,2.

Form the normalized antisymmetric vector

w=\frac{u\otimes v-v\otimes u}{\sqrt2}.

The same condition is equivalent to a vanishing matrix contraction:

\operatorname{Tr}_1\!\left[(T_{Lq}\otimes I)|w\rangle\langle w|\right]=0.

This equation retains the off-diagonal conditions between u and v, as well as the anticoherence of each basis vector.

2. Build a positive four-copy moment

Average the projector of w\otimes w over collective rotations:

X=\int_{SU(2)} U_g^{\otimes4}|w\otimes w\rangle\langle w\otimes w|(U_g^\dagger)^{\otimes4}\,dg. X\succeq0,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}X=1,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}_1\!\left[(T_{L0}\otimes I^{\otimes3})X\right]=0\quad(L=1,2).

Because w=u\wedge v is decomposable, w\wedge w=0. The four-copy vector belongs to the 825-dimensional space S_{(2,2)}(V_{9/2}): it is antisymmetric within each pair, symmetric under exchange of the pairs, and has zero fully antisymmetric component.

Decompose this space into total-spin sectors. Rotational invariance expresses X through positive semidefinite multiplicity blocks B_J. A real basis allows their entrywise real parts to be used without losing positivity, normalization or the necessary contraction equations. The saved highest-weight bases need not be orthogonal: the block expansion is an injective congruence, so positivity is equivalent to positivity of its coefficient blocks. The basis, contraction and trace coefficients are real, which also preserves trace one when taking real parts. There are 169 real symmetric block coordinates, of which 57 are diagonal.

3. An exact positive identity forces zero

The two matrix contraction equations yield 156 linearly independent rational equations. In the supplied basis, let b list the 169 block coordinates. The exact matrix A and a complete integer kernel basis K obey

A b=0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}A=156,\qquad \ker A=\operatorname{im}K,\qquad K\in\mathbb Z^{169\times13}.

The contradiction

\sum_J\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m_J}c_{J\alpha}(B_J)_{\alpha\alpha}=0,\qquad c_{J\alpha}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.

Every diagonal coordinate has a strictly positive coefficient. Since B_J\succeq0, all 57 diagonal entries must vanish. A positive semidefinite matrix with zero diagonal is zero, so every block vanishes and X=0. This contradicts \operatorname{Tr}X=1.

The identity is verified by exact integer multiplication against every column of K. The full integer weights are in the certificate; the highest-weight vectors, selected equations and kernel are in the basis and equation record. No numerical infeasibility result is used in the proof.

Any subspace of dimension greater than two contains a two-dimensional subspace with the same AC property. The exclusion therefore proves an upper bound of one, attained by the state above.

Basis and contraction conventions

Use b_i=\sqrt{\binom9i}\,|i\rangle, with m=i-9/2. Physical four-index amplitudes are antisymmetric within the first and second pairs and symmetric under pair exchange. For a<b<c<d, decomposability gives

x_{ab,cd}-x_{ac,bd}+x_{ad,bc}=0.

The real highest-weight bases and all descendants are reconstructed using exact spin-raising and spin-lowering operators. In this convention the two diagonal tensors can be scaled to

f_1(i)=2i-9,\qquad f_2(i)=3(2i-9)^2-99.

Only necessary diagonal-component contractions are used. The proof does not assume that the unknown subspace has real coefficients or any particular Majorana symmetry.

The spin multiplicities are (m_0,m_2,m_3,m_4,m_5,m_6,m_7,m_8,m_9,m_{10},m_{11},m_{12},m_{13},m_{14},m_{16})=(3,6,2,7,4,7,4,7,3,5,2,3,1,2,1). They account for all 825 dimensions.
