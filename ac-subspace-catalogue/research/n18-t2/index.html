Source and reconstruction

The starting point is John Martin’s numerical example in ACLiege.jl, line 966. Resolving the subspace into Dicke residues modulo three gives dimensions two, one and one. A common rotation aligns a half-turn with coefficient reversal; a unitary basis change selects the displayed even, odd and reflected vectors.

The simple rational probability lists reduce the remaining problem to ten phase variables. High-precision refinement reveals rational squares for all real and imaginary components. The displayed radical formulas are then certified by exact cross-reduction identities, independently of that numerical discovery.

After this common rotation, the numerical and analytical subspace projectors differ by less than in Frobenius norm. This is approximate source association; the original and displayed bases are not claimed to be componentwise equal.
