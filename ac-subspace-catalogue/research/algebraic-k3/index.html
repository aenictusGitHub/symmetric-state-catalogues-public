The condition is on every superposition

Throughout, D_r=|D_n^{(r)}\rangle denotes a normalized Dicke state. An orthonormal basis \{u_1,u_2,u_3\} spans a second-order anticoherent subspace precisely when its two-qubit reduced cross operators obey

R_2(|u_a\rangle\langle u_b|)=\delta_{ab}\frac{I_3}{3}\qquad(a,b=1,2,3).

The identity includes off-diagonal pairs. Consequently every normalized vector in the span has the maximally mixed two-qubit reduction on the symmetric subspace. The verifiers check all nine cross matrices, not only the three basis states.

For coefficients c_{a,r}=\sqrt{\binom nr}\,z_{a,r}, the entries reduce to

\bigl[R_2(|u_a\rangle\langle u_b|)\bigr]_{ij}=\sqrt{\binom2i\binom2j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-2}{\ell}z_{a,i+\ell}\overline{z_{b,j+\ell}},\qquad i,j=0,1,2.

This form turns every required identity into a rational quadratic equation in the real and imaginary coefficient parameters.

Proof of existence

4. Why a tiny residual becomes an exact root

For fifteen and seventeen qubits, numerical searching suggests a center but is not the existence proof. The proof uses a rational polynomial map F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d, an exact rational center x_0, a box radius r, and a rational approximate inverse A of its Jacobian. Define

T(x)=x-AF(x),\qquad B=\{x:\|x-x_0\|_\infty\le r\}.

All following bounds are verified using exact rational arithmetic:

\eta=\|AF(x_0)\|_\infty,\qquad\sup_{x\in B}\|I-A\,DF(x)\|_\infty\le q,\qquad q<1,\qquad\eta+qr<r.
QubitsUnknownsBox radiusDisplacement boundContraction bound
151410^{-45}\eta<10^{-59}q<10^{-40}
172710^{-60}\eta<10^{-90}q<10^{-56}

The mean-value inequality shows that T(B)\subset B and that T is a strict contraction. The Banach fixed-point theorem supplies exactly one fixed point in the box. The verifiers also establish that A is nonsingular, so its fixed point satisfies F(x)=0. The Jacobian is nonsingular throughout the box because \|I-A\,DF(x)\|_\infty<1. This is therefore an isolated algebraic root of a rational polynomial system.

Finally, exact polynomial identities connect the defining equations to every reduced cross operator. At fifteen qubits all 162 real components are rational linear combinations of the fourteen defining equations. At seventeen qubits they reduce to zero or to a signed defining equation. These identities certify an orthonormal basis and anticoherence of its entire span.

Global dimension bounds

5. Why fourteen and fifteen qubits are settled

Let P project onto any k-dimensional second-order AC subspace at spin j=n/2, and put \alpha=j(j+1)/3. Its compressed first and second moments satisfy

PJ_aP=0,\qquad PJ_aJ_bP=\alpha\delta_{ab}P\qquad(a,b=x,y,z).

For any orthonormal basis \{u_b\}_{b=1}^k, the following 4k vectors are mutually orthonormal:

\left\{|u_b\rangle,\frac{J_x|u_b\rangle}{\sqrt\alpha},\frac{J_y|u_b\rangle}{\sqrt\alpha},\frac{J_z|u_b\rangle}{\sqrt\alpha}\right\}_{b=1}^{k}.

They lie in a space of dimension n+1, hence

4k\le n+1.

At fourteen qubits this gives k\le3. This older bound at seventeen gives k\le4. At fifteen it gives k\le4, but equality is impossible.

Excluding dimension four at fifteen qubits

If n=15,k=4, the sixteen spin-generated vectors would form a complete basis. With \Phi(X)=\sum_aJ_aXJ_a and \alpha=85/4, their projectors sum to

I=P+\frac{\Phi(P)}{85/4}.

A rank-L irreducible spin tensor is an eigenoperator of \Phi with eigenvalue 255/4-L(L+1)/2. Every positive-rank coefficient p_{LM} of P must therefore satisfy

\left[4-\frac{2L(L+1)}{85}\right]p_{LM}=0\qquad(1\le L\le15).

The factor could vanish only if L(L+1)=170, but 12\cdot13=156<170<182=13\cdot14. Thus all positive-rank coefficients vanish and P is scalar. Since its trace is four in a sixteen-dimensional space, P=I/4, contradicting P^2=P. Dimension four is excluded; the exact three-dimensional construction proves the maximum.
