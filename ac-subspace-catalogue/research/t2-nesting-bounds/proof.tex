\title{Certified second-order AC subspaces from variable first-order parents}
\author{}\date{}\maketitle

\begin{theorem}
There exist complex second-order anticoherent subspaces of dimension five
at $N=35$ and dimension six at $N=42$. In combination with the established
unrestricted upper bounds,
\[
 \boxed{5\le K(35,2)\le8,\qquad6\le K(42,2)\le9.}
\]
Their Dicke coefficients are specified analytically as rational combinations
of square roots and the unique real zero of an explicit rational quadratic
system in a certified box. Short expressions in radicals and optimality
of either dimension are not asserted.
\end{theorem}

\section{A family of first-order parents}
Let $j=N/2$ and use normalized Dicke states $e_r=|D_N^{(r)}\rangle$, with
$J_ze_r=(j-r)e_r$. Restrict to the parity space
\[
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{even}}=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_2,e_4,\ldots\}.
\]
Since $J_x$ and $J_y$ change excitation parity, their compressions to this
space vanish. A subspace of this parity space is therefore first-order AC
if and only if its $J_z$ compression vanishes.

For $N=35$ the parity space has dimension eighteen and the restricted
$J_z$ has signature $(9,9)$. For $N=42$ these numbers are twenty-two and
$(11,11)$. In both cases there is no zero eigenvalue. Order the positive
and negative magnetic levels so that
\[
 J_z|_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{even}}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}D_+&0\\0&-D_-\end{pmatrix},\qquad D_+,D_->0,
\]
where both blocks have size $d=9$ or $11$ respectively. Every unitary
matrix $T\in U(d)$ gives a first-order parent through the graph
\[
 G_T=\begin{pmatrix}D_+^{-1/2}\\D_-^{-1/2}T\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 V_T=G_T(G_T^\dagger G_T)^{-1/2}.
\]
Indeed $V_T^\dagger V_T=I_d$ and $G_T^\dagger J_zG_T=I-T^\dagger T=0$.
The other two first-order compressions vanish by parity.

Conversely, every $J_z$-isotropic subspace of the parity space is contained
in one of these $d$-dimensional graphs. Its projection onto the positive
levels is injective. Multiplying its positive and negative components by
$D_+^{1/2}$ and $D_-^{1/2}$ gives two isometric copies of the same subspace,
since all polarized $J_z$ matrix elements vanish. The isometry between
these copies extends to a unitary $T$ on the full $d$-dimensional spaces.
This proves the containment claim.

Thus parity-supported second-order construction is equivalent to nesting
inside a \emph{variable} analytical first-order parent from this family.
It does not restrict to one previously selected parent. These parents are
maximal within the parity space; their dimensions nine and eleven are
smaller than the unrestricted first-order maxima twelve and fifteen.
No classification of all first-order subspaces outside this parity space
is assumed.

\section{The complete second-order polynomial system}
Write $c=j(j+1)/3$ and let the $k$ candidate vectors be the columns of $B$.
Their first- and second-rank spin compressions are exactly the conditions
\[
 B^\dagger B=I_k,\quad B^\dagger J_zB=0,\quad
 B^\dagger(J_z^2-cI)B=0,\quad B^\dagger J_+^2B=0.
 \tag{1}
\]
All operators shifting excitation by one, including $J_+$ and
$\{J_z,J_+\}$, already have zero compression by parity. Together with
the Casimir relation and adjoints, (1) is therefore equivalent to the
full second-order conditions, including every cross term.

Let $p=\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ denote the parity-space dimension, and write
\[
 B_{ra}=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\binom Nr}}{2^{e_r}}z_{ra},&r\text{ even},\\
 0,&r\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad z_{ra}=x_{ra}+iy_{ra}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The nonnegative integer exponents $e_r$ are specified in each certificate.
All real and imaginary parts of the following matrices are rational
quadratic polynomials in the $2pk$ real coordinates:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G&=B^\dagger B-I_k,&
 Z&=B^\dagger J_zB/j,\\
 Q&=B^\dagger(J_z^2-cI)B/j^2,&
 H&=B^\dagger J_+^2B/j^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The denominator cancellation in the last matrix follows from
\[
 \sqrt{\binom Nr\binom N{r+2}}
 \sqrt{(r+1)(r+2)(N-r)(N-r-1)}
 =\binom Nr(N-r)(N-r-1).
\]
Use the real diagonals and complex upper triangles of $G,Z,Q$, and all
ordered complex entries of $H$. This gives exactly $5k^2$ real equations.
Multiplication by
\[
 L=3N^2\,2^{2\max_r e_r}
\]
makes every polynomial coefficient integral. The files
\texttt{n35-k5/system.json} and \texttt{n42-k6/system.json} list every
constant and quadratic monomial of these systems in an explicit coordinate
order.

\section{Exact rational existence certificates}
For $N=35$, fix fifty-five of the 180 real coordinates to the rational
values in the certificate and retain 125 unknowns. For $N=42$, fix
eighty-four of 264 coordinates and retain 180 unknowns. These choices
leave square polynomial systems $F:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^m$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(N,k)&\text{real unknowns }m&\text{fixed rational coordinates}&\text{equations}\\\hline
(35,5)&125&55&125\\
(42,6)&180&84&180.
\end{array}
\]
Let $x_0$ be the rational center, $A$ the rational inverse approximation,
and set $\rho=10^{-50}$. All these data are explicitly supplied in
\texttt{certificate.json}; the center denominator is $10^{80}$.
Define
\[
 \varepsilon=\|I-AF'(x_0)\|_\infty,\qquad
 \eta=\|AF(x_0)\|_\infty,\qquad
 M=\|A\|_\infty.
\]
If a polynomial is written $F_a=\gamma_a+\sum_{u\le v}w_{auv}x_ux_v$
in the full coordinate list, with some coordinates fixed, take
\[
 H_*=\max_a 2\!\sum_{\substack{u\le v\\u,v\ \mathrm{unknown}}}|w_{auv}|.
\]
It bounds the Lipschitz constant of the Jacobian in the infinity norm.
The exact integer products and rational comparisons in both certificates
give the following convenient outward bounds:
\[
 \varepsilon<4\cdot10^{-13},\qquad
 \eta<5\cdot10^{-81},\qquad
 q:=\varepsilon+MH_*\rho<4\cdot10^{-13},
\]
\[
 \eta+q\rho<4\cdot10^{-63}<\rho.
 \tag{3}
\]
These displayed bounds are consequences of the exact rational quantities
saved in the reports, not rounded residuals used as an existence test.

For $\mathcal T(x)=x-AF(x)$, equations (3) show that $\mathcal T$ is a
strict contraction mapping the closed box
$\|x-x_0\|_\infty\le\rho$ into itself. Banach's theorem gives its unique
fixed point. Since $\varepsilon<1$, the square matrix $AF'(x_0)$ is
invertible and hence $A$ is invertible; therefore that fixed point is
exactly a zero of $F$. This proves existence of the bases (2).
The exported parameter intervals are checked to be exactly
$[x_{0,u}-\rho,x_{0,u}+\rho]$, and each coefficient formula is associated
with those same coordinates and fixed rational values.

\section{Direct whole-subspace cross-reduction audit}
For any Dicke vector $b$, define
\[
 (M_2(b))_{as}=b_{a+s}
 \sqrt{\frac{\binom2a\binom{N-2}s}{\binom N{a+s}}},
 \qquad0\le a\le2,\quad0\le s\le N-2.
\]
For basis columns $b_i,b_l$, the reduced cross operator is
$R_{il}=M_2(b_i)M_2(b_l)^\dagger$. Direct substitution of (2) reconstructs
all its entries as rational polynomials, apart from the harmless known
factors $\sqrt{\binom2a\binom2b}$.

For example, with $\lambda=N/[4(N-1)]$, direct binomial identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (R_{il})_{00}-\delta_{il}/3
   &=\overline{G_{il}/3+Z_{il}/2+\lambda Q_{il}},\\
 (R_{il})_{11}-\delta_{il}/3
   &=2\overline{G_{il}/6-\lambda Q_{il}},\\
 (R_{il})_{22}-\delta_{il}/3
   &=\overline{G_{il}/3-Z_{il}/2+\lambda Q_{il}},\\
 (R_{il})_{02}&=\lambda\overline{H_{il}},\qquad
 (R_{il})_{20}=\lambda H_{li}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The other four entries vanish by parity. Thus every zero of the complete
system satisfies
\[
 R_{il}=\delta_{il}I_3/3.
\]
The independent verifier reconstructs these polynomial identities for
all 225 ordered complex cross-reduction entries at $(35,5)$ and all 324
at $(42,6)$. It also checks Hermitian completion of the selected moment
equations, every exported coefficient, the isolating intervals, and all
rational contraction inequalities without importing the constructor or
the primary verifier.

Consequently every normalized complex superposition in either span is
second-order AC. The certified lower bounds are five and six. The separate
known inequalities $4k\le N-2$ and $5k\le N+5$ give upper bounds eight and
nine respectively, leaving the intervals stated in the theorem.

\section{Reproducibility and scope}
Run \texttt{verify\_parity\_certificate.py} on either certificate, and
\texttt{verify\_parity\_independently.py} on either example directory.
The complete basis definitions, polynomial systems, rational centers,
inverse matrices, exact bounds and verification reports are retained.
Numerical searches suggested the starting points; they are not used as
existence proofs. No universal saturation family or stronger unrestricted
upper bound follows from these two examples.
