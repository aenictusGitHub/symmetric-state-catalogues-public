Thirteen qubits: first-order maximum dimension is five

A six-dimensional first-order anticoherent subspace is impossible. An exact positive certificate proves the upper bound for arbitrary complex subspaces, and an analytical five-dimensional basis attains it.

1. Keep every cross condition

Suppose six orthonormal vectors span a first-order AC subspace with projector P. The whole-subspace conditions are

PJ_aP=0\qquad(a=x,y,z).

The normalized exterior product is

w=u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_6\in\Lambda^6V_{13/2},\qquad\dim\Lambda^6V_{13/2}=3003.

Its signed omission wedges w_a are orthonormal. Expanding the first tensor factor gives

\operatorname{Tr}_1[(T\otimes I^{\otimes5})|w\rangle\langle w|]=\frac16\sum_{a,b}\langle u_b|T|u_a\rangle|w_a\rangle\langle w_b|.

For each spin operator this matrix vanishes. This retains both diagonal expectations and every off-diagonal condition between basis vectors.

2. Average over rotations

The collective rotational average X of |w\rangle\langle w| must satisfy

X\succeq0,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}X=1,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}_1[(J_z\otimes I^{\otimes5})X]=0.

The complete exterior representation has 24 spin sectors and 151 multiplicity basis vectors. Its invariant blocks have 735 real symmetric coordinates. Taking real parts preserves positivity and the real contraction equations, so the argument applies to arbitrary complex subspaces.

The saved highest-weight bases can be nonorthogonal. Positivity is preserved by the coordinate congruence, and all Gram terms are retained in the trace.

3. An exact positive identity excludes dimension six

Seven hundred sixteen selected physical contraction equations give

Ax=0,\quad A\in\mathbb Z^{716\times735},\quad\operatorname{rank}A=716,\quad\ker A=\operatorname{im}K,\quad K\in\mathbb Z^{735\times19}.

The ranks are certified modulo a prime, and the kernel product is checked exactly over the integers.

The contradiction

\sum_{J,a}c_{Ja}C^J_{aa}=0,\qquad c_{Ja}\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad C^J\succeq0.

All 151 block diagonals have strictly positive integer weights. They must all vanish. Positivity then forces every off-diagonal entry to vanish, giving X=0 in contradiction with \operatorname{Tr}X=1.

The identity annihilates all nineteen kernel columns exactly. The largest weight has 281 digits; all weights are supplied in full. Numerical infeasibility is not used as proof.

An analytical basis attains dimension five

Write D_r=|D_{13}^{(r)}\rangle for normalized Dicke states and define

a=\frac{65}{282},\qquad b=\frac{35}{94},\qquad c=\frac{56}{141},\qquad\zeta=\frac{-21\sqrt{182}+i\sqrt{92794}}{416}.

Here a+b+c=1 and |\zeta|=1. An orthonormal basis is

\begin{aligned}u_1&=\sqrt a\,\zeta D_3-\sqrt b\,D_6+\sqrt c\,D_9,\\u_2&=\sqrt c\,D_4-\sqrt b\,D_7+\sqrt a\,\zeta D_{10},\\u_3&=(D_5+D_8)/\sqrt2,\\u_4&=\sqrt{13/24}\,D_1+\sqrt{11/24}\,D_{13},\\u_5&=\sqrt{11/24}\,D_0-\sqrt{13/24}\,D_{12}.\end{aligned}

All 100 entries of the 25 ordered one-qubit cross reductions are verified exactly:

\operatorname{Tr}_{12}(|u_a\rangle\langle u_b|)=\delta_{ab}\frac{I_2}{2}.

This is the thirteen-qubit specialization of the central-three construction with reflected outer pairs. Every normalized superposition is first-order AC.
