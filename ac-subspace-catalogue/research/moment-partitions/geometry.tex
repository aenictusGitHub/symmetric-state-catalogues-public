\title{Prescribed-moment partitions and overlapping radical compressions}
\author{}\date{}\maketitle
Put $j=N/2$ and $c=j(j+1)/3$. Let $S\subset\{-j,-j+1,\ldots,j\}$
be a set with pairwise distances at least three. On the Dicke coordinate
space supported on $S$, all spin operators shifting the magnetic quantum
number by one or two have zero compression. Hence a matrix $Q$ whose
columns are supported there is an orthonormal AC2 basis precisely when
\[
 Q^*Q=I,\qquad Q^*DQ=0,\qquad Q^*(D^2-cI)Q=0,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(m:m\in S).
\]
These equations include every cross condition, not only the individual
vectors' moments.

\section*{Disjoint convex blocks}
For a block $B\subset S$, nonnegative probabilities $w_m$ give the required
moments exactly when
\[
 \sum_{m\in B}w_m(m,m^2)=(0,c),\qquad\sum_{m\in B}w_m=1.
\]
Disjoint blocks therefore produce orthogonal AC2 vectors
$\sum_{m\in B}\sqrt{w_m}\,|j,m\rangle$. A three-point block with distinct
nodes $a,b,d$ has the unique rational weights
\[
 w_a=\frac{c+bd}{(a-b)(a-d)},\quad
 w_b=\frac{c+ad}{(b-a)(b-d)},\quad
 w_d=\frac{c+ab}{(d-a)(d-b)}.
\]
A two-point block exists precisely when $ab=-c$; its weights are
$w_a=-b/(a-b)$ and $w_b=a/(a-b)$.

Let $M=|S|$, let $d$ be the Tukey depth of $(0,c)$ in the planar set
$\{(m,m^2):m\in S\}$, and let $p$ be the number of pairs with $ab=-c$.
Those pairs are automatically disjoint, since each nonzero node has a
unique possible partner.
\begin{proposition}
The largest number of disjoint prescribed-moment blocks on this fixed
support set is
\[
 \min\left\{d,\left\lfloor\frac{M+p}{3}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Carath\'eodory's theorem reduces each block to a pair or triangle; singletons
cannot represent $(0,c)$ because $c>0$. At most $p$ blocks are pairs, giving
$3k-p\le M$. Each block meets every generic halfplane through the target,
giving $k\le d$. Conversely, remove all $p$ pairs. Each pair contributes
exactly one point to every generic halfplane through the target, so the
remaining depth is $d-p$. The remaining moment points together with the
target are in general position. The prescribed planar Tverberg theorem
(\cite[Theorem 7]{hp}) supplies
$\min\{d-p,\lfloor(M-2p)/3\rfloor\}$ disjoint triangles. Restoring the
pairs attains the upper bound.
\end{proof}
This is a theorem about fixed disjoint support blocks. It is not an
unrestricted bound on AC2 subspaces.

\section*{Allowing overlaps: an exact radical prescription}
Overlapping columns can do better than disjoint blocks. Choose a rational
$\varepsilon$ such that
\[
 a_m=m+\varepsilon(m^2-c)\ne0\quad(m\in S),\qquad b_m=m^2-c.
\]
Use $\varepsilon=0$ if $0\notin S$; otherwise any sufficiently small
nonzero rational number works. Sort the ratios $b_m/a_m$ separately over
the positive and negative $a_m$:
\[
 \alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_u,\qquad\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_v.
\]
\begin{proposition}
There is a $k$-dimensional AC2 subspace on this fixed support set if and
only if $k\le\min(u,v)$ and
\[
 \max(\alpha_i,\beta_i)\le
 \min(\alpha_{u-k+i},\beta_{v-k+i}),\qquad1\le i\le k.
\]
When these rational inequalities hold, its basis can be constructed by
field operations and square roots only.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zero compression of the first diagonal form is injective into its
positive and negative coordinate sectors. After a change of code basis,
every such subspace has a spanning matrix
\[
 F=\begin{pmatrix}A_+^{-1/2}U\\(-A_-)^{-1/2}V\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad U^*U=V^*V=I_k.
\]
The second form has zero compression if and only if
\[
 U^*\operatorname{diag}(\alpha)U
 =V^*\operatorname{diag}(\beta)V.
\]
The Fan--Pall theorem \cite{fanpall} characterizes the possible sorted
compression eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ by
$\alpha_i\le\lambda_i\le\alpha_{u-k+i}$ and the analogous inequalities
for $\beta$. Their intersection is exactly the displayed criterion. One
can take the rational values $\lambda_i=\max(\alpha_i,\beta_i)$.

For completeness, the isometries can be realized by successive
codimension-one compressions. If rational spectra
$x_1<\cdots<x_m$ and $y_1<\cdots<y_{m-1}$ interlace strictly, put
\[
 z_i^2=\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}(x_i-y_r)}{
                  \prod_{s\ne i}(x_i-x_s)}.
\]
These numbers are positive and sum to one. The normalized vectors with
coordinates $z_i/(x_i-y_r)$ form an isometry whose compression spectrum
is $y$. Shared eigenvalues are retained as coordinate vectors, leaving a
strict interlacing problem. To compress directly to a rational spectrum
$\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_k$, choose a first intermediate spectrum
\[
 y_i=\begin{cases}
 x_i,&i\le m-1-k,\\
 \max\{x_i,\lambda_{i-(m-1-k)}\},&i>m-1-k,
 \end{cases}
\]
and iterate. Interlacing holds at each stage. Finally apply ordinary
Gram--Schmidt to $F$. This uses only arithmetic and square roots and
preserves both zero-compression equations. Since the chosen first form
is $D+\varepsilon(D^2-cI)$, the original first and second moment equations
follow.
\end{proof}
The implementation \texttt{geometry\_graph.py} follows this prescription.
For $N=12$, $S=\{6,3,0,-3,-6\}$, it constructs dimension two even though
no two nodes have product $-14$: disjoint blocks on these five nodes have
dimension at most one. Similarly, for $N=21$ the eight nodes
$10.5,7.5,\ldots,-10.5$ support dimension three, whereas disjoint blocks
have dimension at most two. These examples illustrate an improvement of
the partition ansatz, not a new unrestricted catalogue lower bound.

\section*{A sharp asymptotic limitation of all globally separated supports}
The following argument also covers overlapping supports and nonuniform
choices of $S$. It was derived independently of the finite search.
\begin{proposition}
Any AC2 subspace contained in Dicke levels with global spacing at least
three satisfies
\[
 k\le\left\lceil\frac N9\right\rceil.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a sufficiently small positive $\delta$, consider
\[
 q_\delta(m)=(m+j+\delta)
              \left(m-\frac{c}{j+\delta}\right).
\]
Its compression is zero, since its constant term is $-c$ and the code
has first moment zero and second moment $c$. Its first factor is strictly
positive on $[-j,j]$. Choose $\delta$ small enough that its second root
meets no support point, and that the positive support points are exactly
those with $m\ge c/j=(j+1)/3$. The associated diagonal form is strictly
negative on all other support coordinates. Projection of a totally
isotropic subspace into its positive eigenspace is injective. Thus $k$
is at most the number of support levels in $[(j+1)/3,j]$. That interval
has length $(N-1)/3$ and its levels are separated by at least three, so
\[
 k\le\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{9}\right\rfloor+1
 =\left\lceil\frac N9\right\rceil.
\]
\end{proof}
Consequently, this mechanism cannot asymptotically surpass the previously
proved four-band $N/8$ construction. Improving that rate requires
admitting nearby Dicke levels and satisfying their off-diagonal cross
conditions by cancellation or another mechanism.
