\title{A second-order AC subspace need not lie in a largest first-order AC subspace}
\author{}\date{}\maketitle

Let $\mathcal C$ be a $k$-dimensional second-order anticoherent subspace in
spin $j=N/2$, and let $P$ be its orthogonal projector. Put
$c=j(j+1)/3$. Polarization of the anticoherence identities gives
\[
 PJ_aP=0,\qquad PJ_aJ_bP=c\delta_{ab}P.
\]
Consequently $\mathcal C,J_x\mathcal C,J_y\mathcal C,J_z\mathcal C$ are
mutually orthogonal, with dimension $k$ each. Define
\[
 R=I-P-c^{-1}\sum_{a=x,y,z}J_aPJ_a.
\]
This is the orthogonal projector onto their common orthogonal complement,
and has rank $N+1-4k$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact first-order extension criterion]
A first-order AC subspace containing $\mathcal C$ has exactly the form
$\mathcal C\oplus\mathcal D$, where
\[
 \mathcal D\subseteq\operatorname{ran}R,
 \qquad P_{\mathcal D}J_aP_{\mathcal D}=0\quad(a=x,y,z).
\]
In particular, every such parent has dimension at most $N+1-3k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write any parent as $\mathcal C\oplus\mathcal D$ with
$\mathcal D\perp\mathcal C$. The cross compression conditions are
$\langle d,J_a u\rangle=0$ for every $d\in\mathcal D$, $u\in\mathcal C$.
They are equivalent to $\mathcal D\subseteq\operatorname{ran}R$.
The remaining compression conditions are precisely the displayed
first-order conditions within $\mathcal D$. The dimension bound follows
from $\operatorname{rank}R=N+1-4k$.
\end{proof}

\section*{The catalogue's exact $N=18$, $k=4$ example}
Use the four-dimensional radical basis in
\texttt{../n18-t2/basis.json}, already verified to have AC order two.
Here $j=9$, $c=30$, and $\operatorname{rank}R=3$. In the normalized Dicke
basis $e_r=|D_{18}^{(r)}\rangle$, exact arithmetic gives
\[
 \langle e_1,Re_1\rangle=\frac{1496}{3645},\qquad
 \langle e_1,RJ_zRe_1\rangle=\frac{748}{405}\ne0.
\]
The largest possible first-order AC dimension is
$K(18,1)=\lfloor18/3\rfloor+1=7$.
Any seven-dimensional first-order parent of $\mathcal C$ would have to be
$\mathcal C\oplus\operatorname{ran}R$, but the nonzero matrix element
above excludes this immediately. In particular no rotation or basis
change can place this code inside \emph{any} maximum-dimensional
first-order AC subspace.

In fact its largest first-order parent has dimension exactly five.
Define three normalized vectors
\[
 q_+=\frac{Re_1}{\sqrt{\langle e_1,Re_1\rangle}},\qquad
 q_0=\frac{Re_0}{\sqrt{\langle e_0,Re_0\rangle}},\qquad
 q_-=\frac{Re_2}{\sqrt{\langle e_2,Re_2\rangle}}.
\]
They form an orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{ran}R$.
Writing $Q=(q_+,q_0,q_-)$ and $J_+=J_x+iJ_y$, one obtains
\[
 Q^\dagger J_zQ=\frac92
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q^\dagger J_+Q=\frac9{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1&0&0\\0&-i&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are a scaled spin-one triple after basis phases and a reflection
of the generator coordinates, but an elementary proof suffices.
A two-dimensional subspace with zero compression of the first matrix
must contain $(0,1,0)^T$: its projection onto the positive eigenspace
has a nontrivial kernel, and zero quadratic form then forces the negative
component to vanish as well. An orthogonal second vector is $(a,0,b)^T$
with $|a|=|b|\ne0$. Its matrix element with $(0,1,0)^T$ under the second
matrix is $9a/\sqrt2\ne0$. Thus no such two-dimensional first-order
subspace exists in $\operatorname{ran}R$.
The line spanned by $q_0$ is first-order AC, so
\[
 \mathcal C\oplus\operatorname{span}\{q_0\}
\]
is an explicit five-dimensional first-order AC parent, and five is the
largest possible dimension of a first-order parent of this particular
optimal second-order code.

\section*{Scope}
This does not exclude constructing other optimal second-order codes
inside suitably chosen first-order parents. It shows that restricting
to maximum-dimensional first-order parents is not a complete search
strategy. An analytical family attaining $K(N,1)$ is not a classification
of all first-order AC subspaces, nor of those maximal under inclusion.
The supplied verifier checks the source hash, all independent AC2 spin
conditions, $R=QQ^\dagger$, and both displayed compressed matrices with
exact rational and radical arithmetic.
