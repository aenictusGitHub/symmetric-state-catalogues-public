Build second-order AC subspaces inside first-order parents

Fixing an analytical first-order AC subspace removes all three first-moment constraints. The remaining problem involves five quadrupole matrices in a smaller space. This gives explicit analytical constructions, but restricting to maximum-dimensional first-order parents can miss optimal second-order subspaces.

An exact counterexample at 18 qubits. The catalogue’s optimal four-dimensional second-order subspace has a largest containing first-order AC subspace of dimension five. The unrestricted first-order maximum is seven. Thus even searching every seven-dimensional first-order parent would miss this particular optimal second-order subspace.

Further improvements: varying the analytical first-order parents gives certified dimensions five at N = 35 and six at N = 42. A refined radical family gives floor(N/8) − 1 for all N ≥ 40. Read the new lower bounds and constructions ↗

Nesting remains a useful construction method. A complete search must allow parents beyond the known maximum-dimensional families, including first-order parents that are maximal under inclusion at a smaller dimension.
