Constructing second-order AC subspaces

There are explicit constructions valid for every qubit number from six onward, including a four-component family with dimension growing as N/8. Sharper prescriptions can also be certified in individual cases. A construction attaining the maximum dimension for every qubit number has not been proved.

Relation to known constructions

Tetrahedral invariant polynomials provide another general route to AC2 subspaces: take independent homogeneous invariants of degree N, map their coefficients to Dicke amplitudes, and orthonormalize. The catalogue already includes these Molien constructions. They are not a general maximum-dimension theorem. Pereira and Paul-Paddock, Anticoherent Subspaces ↗

Tverberg constructions of permutation-invariant and spin error-correcting codes guarantee common moments between codewords. Second-order anticoherence additionally fixes those common moments to zero dipole and isotropic variance j(j+1)/3. A distance-three code therefore requires this extra check before it can be imported as an AC2 subspace. Aydin, Albert, and Barg, Quantum error correction beyond SU(2) ↗

The separated-triple and adjacent-triangle constructions above impose the required AC moments explicitly, and their proofs include every cross term. They are catalogue calculations, distinct from a claim that arbitrary error-correcting codes are anticoherent.
