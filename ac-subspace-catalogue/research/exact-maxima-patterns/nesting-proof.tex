\section*{A dimension obstruction to nesting AC subspaces}
\begin{theorem}
Let $1\le s$ and $2s\le N$. Suppose that
$\mathcal C\subset\mathcal A\subset V_{N/2}$, where $\mathcal A$ is
$s$-anticoherent of dimension $d$ and $\mathcal C$ is $2s$-anticoherent
of dimension $k$. Then
\[
\boxed{d+s(s+2)k\le N+1.}
\]
Anticoherence here is a whole-subspace condition: every normalized vector
in the indicated subspace is anticoherent to the required order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose irreducible spin tensors $T_{\ell m}$ on $V_{N/2}$ normalized by
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(T_{\ell m}^{\dagger}T_{\ell' m'})
 =\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'},\qquad
T_{00}=\frac{I}{\sqrt{N+1}}.
\]
Let $P_A$ and $P_C$ be the two orthogonal projectors. Anticoherence and
polarization give
\[
 P_A T_{\ell m}P_A=0\quad(1\le\ell\le s).
\]
The product $T_{\ell m}^{\dagger}T_{\ell' m'}$ has spin-tensor ranks
at most $\ell+\ell'\le2s$. Its compression to $\mathcal C$ therefore
retains only its scalar part, giving
\[
 P_C T_{\ell m}^{\dagger}T_{\ell' m'}P_C
 =\frac{\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'}}{N+1}P_C
 \qquad(1\le\ell,\ell'\le s).
\]
For an orthonormal basis $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ of $\mathcal C$, the vectors
\[
 \sqrt{N+1}\,T_{\ell m}u_a,
 \qquad 1\le\ell\le s,\quad -\ell\le m\le\ell,\quad 1\le a\le k,
\]
are mutually orthonormal. Moreover they are orthogonal to
$\mathcal A$, since $u_a\in\mathcal A$ and
$P_A T_{\ell m}P_A=0$. They span a space of dimension
$k\sum_{\ell=1}^s(2\ell+1)=ks(s+2)$ in $\mathcal A^{\perp}$.
Adding the dimension $d$ proves the inequality.
\end{proof}

\section*{An exact seven-qubit counterexample to maximum-parent nesting}
The catalogue proves $K(7,1)=3$ and $K(7,2)=2$. For example, a
second-order subspace is spanned by the orthonormal Dicke-basis vectors
\[
 u=\sqrt{\frac3{10}}\,|D_7^{(0)}\rangle+
      \sqrt{\frac7{10}}\,|D_7^{(5)}\rangle,
 \qquad
 v=\sqrt{\frac7{10}}\,|D_7^{(2)}\rangle-
      \sqrt{\frac3{10}}\,|D_7^{(7)}\rangle.
\]
Each has $\langle J_z\rangle=0$ and
$\langle J_z^2\rangle=21/4=j(j+1)/3$. All degree-one cross
compressions vanish by support separation; the only potentially nonzero
raising/lowering degree-two cross term cancels between the transitions
$2\to0$ and $7\to5$, whose ladder factors are both $\sqrt{84}$.
Together with the Casimir identity, this verifies second-order
anticoherence on the whole span. The standard bound $4k\le N+1$
proves its optimality.

If any two-dimensional second-order subspace were contained in a
first-order parent of dimension $d$, the theorem with $s=1$ would give
$d+3\cdot2\le8$, hence $d\le2$. It therefore cannot be contained in
\emph{any} maximum-dimensional first-order subspace, since those have
dimension three. In fact every two-dimensional second-order subspace
at $N=7$ is already inclusion-maximal among first-order subspaces.

This result constrains nesting constructions. It does not by itself
improve an unrestricted upper bound on $K(N,2s)$, since one may always
choose the parent $\mathcal A=\mathcal C$.
