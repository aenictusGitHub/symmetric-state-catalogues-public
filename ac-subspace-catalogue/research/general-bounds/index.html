General upper bounds on subspace dimension

Sharper second-order result: the new auxiliary-spin inertia theorem gives for and every order at least two. Proof and improved entries ↗

A single tensor argument bounds the dimension of every pure anticoherent subspace, for any qubit number and AC order. Exact equality tests strengthen the bound when all tensor images would fill the output space.

GENERAL THEOREM · ARBITRARY COMPLEX SUBSPACESk_{\max}(N,2r+1)\le\left\lfloor\frac{N}{(r+1)(r+2)}\right\rfloork_{\max}(N,2r)\le\begin{cases}0,&N<(r+1)^2,\\\left\lfloor\dfrac{N+1}{(r+1)^2}\right\rfloor,&N\ge(r+1)^2.\end{cases}

For odd order, r\ge0; for even order, r\ge1. Assume 1\le t\le N. These are upper bounds; equality is not guaranteed. Stronger existing certificates remain in force.

1. The general dimension argument

Let P project onto a k-dimensional t-AC subspace. For every operator whose spin-tensor ranks are at most t, the full cross conditions give

PAP=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}A}{N+1}P.

Choose an integer 0\le h\le t and tensor maps from the symmetric N-qubit space to the symmetric N-h-qubit space. Use the Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal maps of ranks

\ell=h/2,h/2+1,\ldots\le t/2.

A product of two selected maps has input tensor rank at most t. Therefore, for any orthonormal subspace basis,

\langle A_{\ell q}u_a,A_{\ell^{\prime} q^{\prime}}u_b\rangle=\frac{\delta_{ab}\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{qq^{\prime}}}{N+1}.

There are m_h(m_h+h) maps, where m_h=\lfloor(t-h)/2\rfloor+1. All their images, for all basis vectors, are nonzero and mutually orthogonal. The output space has dimension N-h+1, so

\boxed{k\,m_h(m_h+h)\le N-h+1\qquad(0\le h\le t).}

This is the complete bound family. Optimizing over h gives the formulas above.

The choice h=t recovers the Schmidt bound; h=0 recovers the ordinary tensor-Gram bound. At odd order, h=1 gives the stronger count. At even order, h=2 improves the pure-state threshold.

Useful special cases

t=1:\ 2k\le N;\qquad t=2:\ 4k\le N+1;\qquad t=3:\ 6k\le N;t=4:\ 9k\le N+1;\qquad t=5:\ 12k\le N;\qquad t=7:\ 20k\le N.

Every nonzero pure t-AC state must also satisfy the compact necessary condition

\boxed{(t+2)^2\le4N+1.}

2. When equality is impossible

At equality, the orthogonal images form a complete basis. Their sum of projectors is the identity. The corresponding rotation-covariant channel then forces every projector multipole with a nonzero channel multiplier to vanish.

The multipliers are exact finite sums of Wigner 6j symbols. The complete formulas and their terminating rational-polynomial forms are in the LaTeX proof. All zero tests used by the catalogue are exact.

Even orders

If k(r+1)^2=N+1, at least one rank 2r+1\le L\le N must have zero channel multiplier. Otherwise the projector would be a scalar multiple of the identity and could not be a nontrivial projection.

For fourth order, put Q=N(N+2),\ x=L(L+1). A necessary equality condition is

10x^2+(16-12Q)x+3Q(Q-3)=0.

Odd orders

There is one extra invisible rank, L=N. If no other allowed rank has zero multiplier, equality would force P=\alpha I+X_N. Time-reversed vectorization of X_N gives a pure symmetric 2N-qubit state of AC order N-1. The pure-state bound above would require

(N+1)^2\le8N+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N(N-6)\le0.

This rules out such projectors for every N\ge7. Thus, at odd-order equality, a zero multiplier at a rank below N is necessary.

Third order has a simple arithmetic test. If 6\mid N and N\ge12, equality requires an integer 4\le L\le N-1 satisfying

2L(L+1)=(N-1)(N+2).

When no such integer exists, k_{\max}(N,3)\le N/6-1. This settles the maximum at twelve and eighteen qubits using existing exact witnesses.

An additional pure-state equality test

For k=1 at odd-order equality, the channel must also vanish at the next rank L=t+1. Otherwise the state would have higher AC order and violate the even-order pure-state bound. At N=20,t=7, this next multiplier is nonzero, excluding the last possible pure state.

A zero channel multiplier is only a necessary condition. It does not certify the existence of a saturating subspace.
