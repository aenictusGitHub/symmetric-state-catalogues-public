Eleven qubits: first-order maximum dimension is four

A five-dimensional first-order anticoherent subspace is impossible. An exact positive certificate proves the upper bound for arbitrary complex subspaces; the existing Julia basis attains dimension four.

Full compressed-spin conditions→ antisymmetric basis vector→ positive invariant moment→ exact contradiction

1. Preserve every cross condition

Suppose an orthonormal basis u_1,\ldots,u_5 spans a first-order AC subspace, with projector P. The condition for the whole subspace is

PJ_aP=0\qquad(a=x,y,z).

Form its normalized exterior product

w=u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_5\in\Lambda^5 V_{11/2},\qquad\dim\Lambda^5 V_{11/2}=792.

The five signed omission wedges w_a are orthonormal. Expanding the first tensor factor gives, for any spin operator T,

\operatorname{Tr}_1[(T\otimes I^{\otimes4})|w\rangle\langle w|]=\frac15\sum_{a,b}\langle u_b|T|u_a\rangle|w_a\rangle\langle w_b|=0.

This matrix identity retains the off-diagonal conditions between distinct basis vectors, as well as each vector’s vanishing spin expectation.

2. Average over rotations

Let X be the collective rotational average of |w\rangle\langle w|. It is supported on the exterior space and must satisfy

X\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{Tr}X=1,\quad\operatorname{Tr}_1[(J_z\otimes I^{\otimes4})X]=0.

The complete spin decomposition has 17 sectors and 49 multiplicity basis vectors. Its invariant positive blocks have 111 real symmetric coordinates. Taking the real part of those blocks preserves positivity and the real contraction equations, so this reduction also covers arbitrary complex original subspaces.

The saved highest-weight bases need not be orthogonal. Their coefficient blocks are positive by an invertible congruence, and their Gram matrices are retained in the trace. The downloadable proof specifies these conventions.

3. The exact positive identity

One hundred selected physical contraction equations are independent over the rationals. With x\in\mathbb R^{111} denoting the block coordinates, the saved integer matrices satisfy

Ax=0,\quad\operatorname{rank}A=100,\quad\ker A=\operatorname{im}K,\quad K\in\mathbb Z^{111\times11}.

The contradiction

\sum_{J,a}c_{Ja}C^J_{aa}=0,\qquad c_{Ja}\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad C^J\succeq0.

Every one of the 49 block diagonals has a strictly positive integer weight. All diagonals must therefore vanish. Positivity forces every off-diagonal entry to vanish too, giving X=0, in contradiction with \operatorname{Tr}X=1.

The identity is checked exactly against all eleven columns of the kernel. The largest weight has 121 digits; the full integers are available in the certificate. Neither a floating-point sign test nor numerical infeasibility is used as proof.

An exact four-dimensional basis attains the bound

Writing D_r=|D_{11}^{(r)}\rangle for normalized Dicke states, the existing Julia example is

\begin{aligned}u_1&=\sqrt{7/10}\,D_4+\sqrt{3/10}\,D_9,\\u_2&=\alpha D_0+\frac{\sqrt3}{5}D_5-\frac7{10}D_{10},\\u_3&=-\frac7{10}D_1+\frac{\sqrt3}{5}D_6+\alpha D_{11},\\u_4&=\sqrt{3/10}\,D_2+\sqrt{7/10}\,D_7,\end{aligned}\alpha=\frac{36+5i\sqrt{789}}{70\sqrt{11}},\qquad |\alpha|^2=\frac{39}{100}.

The simplified coefficient is exactly the one in the supplied Julia source. All 64 entries of the sixteen ordered one-qubit cross reductions are verified:

\operatorname{Tr}_{10}(|u_a\rangle\langle u_b|)=\delta_{ab}\frac{I_2}{2}.

This establishes the matching lower bound.
