Eight qubits: every AC order settled

1. Exact witnesses

All Dicke states below are normalized, with the superscript counting excitations. The following orthonormal basis spans a first-order AC subspace:

\begin{aligned}|u_1\rangle&=|D_8^{(4)}\rangle,\\|u_2\rangle&=\sqrt{\frac35}|D_8^{(2)}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac25}|D_8^{(7)}\rangle,\\|u_3\rangle&=\sqrt{\frac25}|D_8^{(1)}\rangle-\sqrt{\frac35}|D_8^{(6)}\rangle.\end{aligned}

The supports are disjoint and every magnetic mean vanishes. All compressed ladder matrix elements vanish; the only possible cross term cancels between two transitions with equal ladder factors and opposite amplitude products. Thus PJ_xP=PJ_yP=PJ_zP=0 for the projector onto this span, proving first-order anticoherence of every superposition.

For orders two and three, the single normalized vector

|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{\frac5{24}}\bigl(|D_8^{(0)}\rangle+|D_8^{(8)}\rangle\bigr)-\sqrt{\frac7{12}}|D_8^{(4)}\rangle

has both R_2(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=I_3/3 and R_3(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=I_4/4. The support gaps remove off-diagonal moments through rank three, reflection symmetry removes odd diagonal moments, and \langle J_z^2\rangle=20/3. The complete reduced matrices are also checked exactly by the verifier.

Global exclusion

2. Why dimension four is impossible at first order

The one-qubit Schmidt-map bound gives 2k\le8. It remains to rule out equality. Suppose P projects onto a four-dimensional first-order AC subspace.

Step 1 · Saturation fixes the seven-qubit reduction

Write an orthonormal basis in the 1\mid7 bipartition as

|u_a\rangle=|0\rangle|v_{a0}\rangle+|1\rangle|v_{a1}\rangle,\qquad a=1,2,3,4.

The full cross conditions R_1(|u_a\rangle\langle u_b|)=\delta_{ab}I_2/2 imply

\langle v_{b\beta}|v_{a\alpha}\rangle=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

There are eight mutually orthogonal vectors \sqrt2\,v_{a\alpha} in the eight-dimensional symmetric seven-qubit space. They form a complete orthonormal basis, so

R_7(P)=\frac{I_8}{2}.

Step 2 · Only the highest multipole can remain

Partial trace is covariant under rotations. In Hilbert–Schmidt normalized spin tensors it preserves every rank below eight with a nonzero coefficient and annihilates rank eight. Therefore

P=\frac49I_9+X,\qquad X=X^\dagger,\qquad X\text{ has only tensor rank }8.

The projector equation P^2=P gives

X^2=\frac19X+\frac{20}{81}I_9,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}X^2=\frac{20}{9}.

Step 3 · Turn the operator into a symmetric pure state

In the spin-four magnetic basis, let F_{mn}=(-1)^{4-m}\delta_{m,-n} be the linear part of time reversal, and define

|\Psi\rangle=\frac{\operatorname{vec}(XF)}{\sqrt{20/9}},\qquad\operatorname{vec}(A)=\sum_{m,n}A_{mn}|4,m\rangle\otimes|4,n\rangle.

The map X\mapsto\operatorname{vec}(XF) carries an operator of tensor rank eight to total spin eight. This is the maximal-spin sector of two spin-four systems, hence the fully symmetric sixteen-qubit space. Its eight-qubit reduction is

R_8(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)=\frac{X^2}{20/9}=\frac{I_9}{9}+\frac{X}{20}.

Tracing one further qubit annihilates X, leaving

R_7(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)=\frac{I_8}{8}.

Thus the hypothetical four-dimensional subspace would create a pure sixteen-qubit state of AC order seven.

Why the vectorization lies in the maximal-spin sector

For a spin rotation D, the time-reversal matrix obeys DFD^{\mathsf T}=F. Therefore

(D\otimes D)\operatorname{vec}(XF)=\operatorname{vec}(DXD^\dagger F).

This is an equivariant isometry from operator space to the tensor-product space: rank-L operator tensors become total-spin-L vectors. Rank eight is the maximal total spin of two spin-four representations. Equivalently, its highest-weight operator maps to |4,4\rangle\otimes|4,4\rangle and the remaining components are generated by lowering. The certificate checks all seventeen rank-eight components explicitly.

Step 4 · An exact positivity contradiction

For any pure spin-eight state, define the nonnegative squared multipole lengths a_L=\sum_M|\langle\Psi|T_{LM}|\Psi\rangle|^2 and the nonnegative total-spin probabilities p_J=\|\Pi_J(|\Psi\rangle\otimes|\Psi\rangle)\|^2. Exact recoupling, under a_1=\cdots=a_7=0, forces

\boxed{0=100+136p_0+115a_9.}

The right side is strictly positive. A pure seventh-order state on sixteen qubits cannot exist, so the original four-dimensional first-order subspace cannot exist.

Complete recoupling identity and independent certificate

Only even J=0,2,\ldots,16 occur in two identical copies. In the normalized tensor convention,

a_L=\sum_{J\in\{0,2,\ldots,16\}}(2L+1)\begin{Bmatrix}8&8&J\\8&8&L\end{Bmatrix}p_J,\qquad\sum_Jp_J=1.

The unconditional rational identity is

0=100+136p_0+115a_9+\sum_{L=1}^7c_La_L,
(c_1,\ldots,c_7)=\left(\frac{2567}2,\frac{969}2,-323,-969,-\frac{28747}{26},-\frac{22287}{26},-\frac{4370}{13}\right).

It is checked coefficientwise in all nine even total-spin sectors. A separate positive certificate gives, under the same low-rank conditions,

0=205620+24871p_2+1292646p_{12}.

The verifier independently reconstructs all 153 recoupling coefficients using rational highest-weight sums, without a Wigner-symbol library.

Proved first-order maximum
\boxed{k_{\max}(8,1)=3}.

Global exclusion

3. Why a second-order AC plane is impossible

Let P now project onto a hypothetical two-dimensional second-order AC subspace at spin j=4. Its compressed spin moments satisfy

PJ_aP=0,\qquad PJ_aJ_bP=\frac{20}{3}\delta_{ab}P.

The two basis vectors and their six normalized spin descendants are eight mutually orthonormal vectors. They leave a one-dimensional orthogonal complement in the nine-dimensional spin space. Define

Q=I-P-\frac3{20}\Phi(P),\qquad\Phi(P)=\sum_{a=x,y,z}J_aPJ_a.

Then Q=|q\rangle\langle q| is a pure-state projector and PQ=0. Decompose operators into irreducible ranks. Since

\Phi(X_L)=\left(20-\frac{L(L+1)}2\right)X_L,

the non-scalar multipoles obey

P_L=\beta_LQ_L,\qquad\beta_L=\frac{40}{3L(L+1)-160}\quad(1\le L\le8).

All denominators are nonzero. At rank zero set \beta_0=2. With a_L=\|Q_L\|_{\rm HS}^2, the pure complementary state satisfies

a_0=\frac19,\quad a_1=a_2=0,\quad X:=\sum_{L=0}^{8}\beta_La_L=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=0,\quad Y:=\sum_{L=0}^{8}\beta_L^2a_L=\operatorname{Tr}(P^2)=2.

The exact two-copy recoupling identity below then reduces to

\boxed{0=5831+217971a_5.}

This is impossible because a_5\ge0. No second-order AC subspace can have dimension two or more.

Complete second-order recoupling identity

For two identical spin-four copies, J\in\{0,2,4,6,8\} and

a_L=\sum_{J\in\{0,2,4,6,8\}}(2L+1)\begin{Bmatrix}4&4&J\\4&4&L\end{Bmatrix}p_J.

The five total-spin coefficient checks give the identity

\begin{aligned}20925216a_5={}&10544114+\frac{5610400200}{121}a_1-\frac{315270952920}{5041}a_2\\&+25055422X-5551945Y.\end{aligned}

Substituting a_1=a_2=X=0,\ Y=2 and dividing by 96 gives the positive contradiction. The verifier reconstructs all 45 rational recoupling coefficients independently from highest-weight sums.

The explicit third-order state above attains dimension one for both orders. Every third-order subspace is also second-order, hence

Proved second- and third-order maxima
\boxed{k_{\max}(8,2)=k_{\max}(8,3)=1}.

4. No pure state reaches order four

For a pure eight-qubit state, the already established exact two-copy identity is

p_0=-\frac4{27}-a_1+\frac{11}{21}a_2+\frac{11}{14}a_3+\frac{143}{126}a_4.

Fourth-order anticoherence sets a_1=a_2=a_3=a_4=0 and forces p_0=-4/27, contradicting positivity of the singlet probability. Higher orders imply fourth order, so no nonzero AC subspace exists at any t\ge4.

General consequence

5. A sharper first-order bound for even sizes

Even-qubit upper bound
\boxed{k_{\max}(N,1)\le\frac N2-1\qquad(N\ge8\text{ even}).}

The eight-qubit case was proved above. For every even N\ge10, an elementary dimension count after the same vectorization rules out saturation of the Schmidt bound.

Suppose a first-order AC subspace had k=N/2. As before, its projector satisfies R_{N-1}(P)=I_N/2 and therefore

P=\alpha I_{N+1}+X,\qquad\alpha=\frac{N}{2(N+1)},\qquad X\text{ has only rank }N.
X^2=\frac{X}{N+1}+\frac{N(N+2)}{4(N+1)^2}I_{N+1},\qquad\operatorname{Tr}X^2=\frac{N(N+2)}{4(N+1)}.

The normalized vector \operatorname{vec}(XF) is a pure symmetric state on 2N qubits with

\rho_N=\frac{I_{N+1}}{N+1}+\frac4{N(N+2)}X,\qquad\rho_{N-1}=\frac{I_N}{N}.

It would thus be (N-1)-AC. The spin-tensor descendants of ranks zero through N/2-1 are nonzero and mutually orthogonal, because products of these tensors have rank at most N-2. Their number must fit in the (2N+1)-dimensional state space:

\sum_{L=0}^{N/2-1}(2L+1)=\frac{N^2}{4}\le2N+1.

This inequality fails for every even N\ge10. Saturation is impossible, leaving the strict integer bound k\le N/2-1.

Partial-trace coefficient used in the argument

For Hilbert–Schmidt normalized tensors, the one-qubit partial trace between symmetric spaces is

R_{N-1}(T_{LM}^{(N)})=\frac{\sqrt{(N-L)(N+L+1)}}{N}\,T_{LM}^{(N-1)}\qquad(0\le L\le N-1).

Every displayed coefficient is nonzero. Rank N is annihilated because the smaller spin space has no such operator rank. The formula follows by applying partial trace to the highest component proportional to J_+^L and then using rotational covariance.

Two further maxima are settled. Existing exact first-order constructions attain dimensions four at ten qubits and five at twelve qubits. Matching the new upper bounds gives

\boxed{k_{\max}(10,1)=4,\qquad k_{\max}(12,1)=5}.
