1. Straight lower-q boundary, 1/6<=x<=1/3
-----------------------------------------

 U=1/2-(ell-h)^2/3-h^2,
 n0=n4=(ell-h)/sqrt(6), n2=h,

symmetric seven-by-seven Hodge matrix K(n). It is the matrix in
output/pdf/assets/N6_complete_paired_face_20261021.tex,

The source construction proves that K(n) has a physical unit eigenvector
with eigenvalue +1 and maps to

In particular the entire trace is the sharp lower-q edge of the complete
paired-face image, not an inferred convex segment. The elementary condition
ell^2-ell=r verifies the real-Hodge compatibility relation. Its real square
roots are feasible because
 U=-(2ell^2-2ell-1)(4ell^4-12ell^3+12ell^2-6ell+3)/6>=0.
The first factor is 2r-1<=0. With v=ell-1<1/2, the second is
4v^4+4v^3-2v+1>0.

For a completely explicit finite formula put B=K(n)-I_7 and
 C_k=(-1)^(j+k) det B_(delete row j, delete column k), j=2,

High-precision cofactor evaluation is used only to obtain browser amplitudes;
there is no optimizer or numerical eigenvector in the construction.

2. Curved odd-C2 boundary
-------------------------
For -1<=s<=3 define A=sqrt(3+sqrt(9-s^2)) and
 |P_s>=(A D1+sqrt(10)D3+(s/A)D5)/4.
Since A^2+s^2/A^2=6, the state has norm1. Its transverse dipole vanishes,
<Jz>=sqrt(9-s^2)/4, and Theta=-(s+5)/8. Its quadrupole has
 Qzz=-5/2, Qxx+Qyy=5/2,
 Qxx-Qyy=(5sqrt(3)/4)(A+s/A),
with other entries zero. Hence
 ||Q||_F^2=75(5+s)/16.
Using x=(2/3)|<J>|^2, y=5+(4/15)||Q||_F^2 and
z=8+2x-8q gives exactly
 x=(9-s^2)/24,
 y=(45+5s)/4,
 z=(135-30s-5s^2)/24.
These satisfy Delta=0 and (4y-45)^2+600x=225. The completed paired-face
proof establishes the entire indicated ellipse as sharp and attained.
The lower branch -1<=s<=0 joins the straight real-Hodge branch at s=-1;
the upper branch 0<=s<=3 reaches the GHZ sector tuple at s=3.
Samples s=-1,-3/4,-1/2,-1/4,0,1/2,1,3/2,2,5/2.
