Symmetric sector atlas — state and constellation conventions
===========================================================

1. State coefficients

All state records use the normalized Dicke basis:
  |psi> = sum(k=0..N) c_k |D_N^(k)>.
Here |D_N^(k)> is the equally weighted, normalized superposition of all
computational basis strings containing exactly k occurrences of |1>.
The JSON representation of c_k is [real part, imaginary part].
There is no hidden binomial weight in the norm: sum_k |c_k|^2=1.
An overall phase is physically irrelevant. The library normalizes its input.

2. Sector lengths without constructing a 2^N x 2^N density matrix

For an s-qubit reduction, form the (s+1) x (N-s+1) matrix
  B[a,b] = c_(a+b) sqrt(C(s,a) C(N-s,b) / C(N,a+b)).
The reduced density matrix in the normalized s-qubit Dicke basis is B B^dagger.
This follows directly by splitting every Dicke state between the two groups of
qubits. Its purity is P_s=sum_(a,a') |(B B^dagger)[a,a']|^2.

Permutation symmetry and Pauli orthogonality imply
  2^s P_s = sum(k=0..s) C(s,k) S_k/C(N,k),  S_0=1.
Indeed, each k-body Pauli correlation contributes to C(N-k,s-k) identical
s-qubit reductions out of C(N,s). Inverting this triangular identity gives
all sectors, in the manuscript normalization. sectorLengths returns S_1..S_N.
For pure states sum_k S_k=2^N-1, excluding S_0.

3. Majorana constellation and its direction

The displayed points are the Bloch vectors of the constituent states in a
symmetrized product, not the antipodal dual constellation. If
  |v_i> = a_i |0> + b_i |1>,
then their symmetrized product has Dicke amplitudes proportional to
  c_k = e_k(a_1,b_1;...;a_N,b_N)/sqrt(C(N,k)),
where e_k is the coefficient of t^k in product_i (a_i+b_i t).

Consequently the roots of
  M(z) = sum(k=0..N) (-1)^k sqrt(C(N,k)) c_k z^(N-k)
are z_i=b_i/a_i. The Bloch vector of the corresponding constituent is
  (2 Re z_i, 2 Im z_i, 1-|z_i|^2)/(1+|z_i|^2).
The root zero is the north pole |0>; an infinite root is the south pole |1>.
Multiplicities are retained. A coherent state has N coincident stars.
A Dicke state |D_N^(k)> has N-k stars at the north pole and k at the south pole.

4. Numerical safeguards and independent audit

The browser library is dependency-free. It removes exact/tiny pole factors,
recognizes coherent states from their one-qubit purity, and uses square-free
polynomial factors plus a centered and scaled Aberth root iteration. A proposed
square-free factorization is accepted only after reconstructing the quantum
state independently from its spinors. Otherwise a direct root solve is used.
If an affine root chart is poorly conditioned, a collective SU(2) rotation
chooses a better polynomial chart. The resulting stars are rotated back before
display, so this internal numerical choice does not change their orientation.
Near a pole, the larger spinor component fixes the gauge to avoid cancellation.

Every constellation reports reconstruction fidelity, homogeneous polynomial
residual, and a reliability flag. These numerical diagnostics do not turn a
sampled state family or a rendered mesh into an exact boundary proof.

The reproducible test test_majorana_math.cjs checks 455 states (2,054 stars):
every Dicke state for N=3..6, GHZ states, the six-star octahedron, arbitrary
complex states, known spinor constellations with repeated/polar stars, and
ill-conditioned affine charts with leading amplitudes from 10^-2 to 10^-14.
For 39 states an independent full tensor-product Pauli enumeration checks every
sector, rather than repeating the reduced-purity implementation.

Recorded audit:
  minimum reconstruction fidelity: 0.9999999999999993
  maximum homogeneous polynomial residual: 7.21e-11
  maximum sector error against full Pauli oracle: 1.57e-13
  maximum known-star matching error: 1.14e-12

The audit JSON contains the exact observed values for this implementation.

The separate audit-atlas.cjs independently recomputes all 12,481 state records
in small-n.json and n6.json using this browser implementation. It checks 948
stratified Majorana constellations (including every named state), all stored
sector tuples, the refined four-copy bound on N=6 witnesses, and every N=5
boundary branch and junction against its explicit state family. The recorded
atlas audit has no failures; its maximum stored-sector error is 1.85e-12 and
its minimum constellation reconstruction fidelity is 0.9999999999999994.
This consistency audit does not re-prove the N=5 global minimization theorem:
that claim is sourced to the dedicated completed five-qubit proof.
