02 / Reading the constellation

Multiplicity and anticoherence

One state, n Majorana points

A pure symmetric n-qubit state has n Majorana points on the Bloch sphere, counted with repetitions. Coincident points form a cluster. Its multiplicity is the number of points in that cluster.

The full partition records every cluster, sorted by multiplicity. The catalogue fixes this entire partition and maximizes the AC order over all compatible geometries.

Every order through t

A pure symmetric state is t-anticoherent when its spin moments through order t are independent of direction. Its reduced state satisfies

A representative of order t also witnesses every lower order. The reported maximum concerns all states in the partition; a displayed witness may have a lower order.

A partition fixes multiplicities, not geometry

The same partition can describe many different constellations and, when there are four or more distinct points, different SLOCC classes. Invertible collective filters preserve the partition but generally change anticoherence. A theorem establishing an AC representative does not make every state with that partition AC.

For exact representatives, multiplicities and AC claims rely on their certificates. Liège library AC orders are checked numerically. Original source constellations are grouped by coordinate proximity; where a refined vector has a root certificate, its full partition is certified for the saved coefficients. Small AC residuals do not prove exact anticoherence or optimality.

03 / Why the maximum is sharp

Bounds and proofs

An explicit state or an existence theorem supplies a lower bound. A proven maximum also requires an upper bound valid for every pure symmetric state with exactly the same multiplicity partition.
