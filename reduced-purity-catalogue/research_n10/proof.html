The f vectors are quartic spin covariants with phase charges 0, 2 and 4. Each is formed from highest-weight polynomials and their normalized lowering sequences. The verifier checks these relations and verifies that the selected linear combinations vanish at the corresponding attaining state. In z coordinates that state is proportional to (1, α, −1) on levels 1, 5 and 9, with all other levels zero.

The complete holomorphic quartic basis has dimension 1001. The verifier checks its independence modulo a prime, weight by weight. For a spin of multiplicity m, Schur's lemma gives m(m+1)/2 real symmetric invariant contractions. The resulting real invariant space of bidegree (4,4), fixed by formal conjugation, has dimension 130.

Both sides of the identity vanish at the attaining state, and both derivatives vanish along the normalized Dicke family. Vanishing of the quartic covariants gives these conditions on the right. Exact substitution and differentiation give them on the left. These are independent linear conditions because n⁴ is constant on the normalized family and the purity of the other reduction has nonzero derivative there. The constrained invariant space therefore has dimension 128.

The verifier evaluates 128 selected Gram coordinates at 128 Gaussian-integer states, dividing each evaluation by n⁴. The resulting square map B is invertible because a supplied rational approximate inverse C satisfies the rigorous bound below. The exact correction to the rational Gram seed is determined by its exact evaluation residual e.

Invertibility gives injectivity on the 128-dimensional constrained invariant space, so the correction proves the full polynomial identity. To check positivity, the verifier subtracts from each seed block a diagonal shift equal to its size times the bound on the entrywise correction. Outward-rounded interval elimination certifies positive pivots in every shifted block. Each exact corrected Gram matrix is therefore positive definite. Dividing the identity by n⁴ proves the claimed global bounds.
