Exact achievable purity inequalities

For each size, every pair satisfying the stated conditions is attained by a pure symmetric state.

Two qubits

$$\frac12\le P_1\le1,\qquad P_2=1.$$

The one-qubit density matrix has purity at least $1/2$. The symmetric family $\sqrt{\lambda}|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-\lambda}|11\rangle$ spans the interval because $P_1=\lambda^2+(1-\lambda)^2$ for $1/2\le\lambda\le1$.

Three qubits

$$\frac12\le P_1\le1,\qquad P_2=P_1.$$

Four qubits

$$\frac12\le P_1\le1,\qquad \frac{4P_1-1}{3}\le P_2\le P_1.$$

Five qubits

$$\frac12\le P_1\le1,\qquad L(P_1)\le P_2\le P_1.$$

Writing $u=P_1$ and $u_c=(8221-984\sqrt5)/11881$, the lower boundary is

$$ L(u)= \begin{cases} u-\dfrac14+\dfrac{\Phi(p)}{40}, & \dfrac12\le u\le u_c,\quad U(p)=5\sqrt{2u-1},\\[2mm] \dfrac{19u-4-\sqrt{2u-1}}{16}, & u_c\le u\le\dfrac{17}{25},\\[2mm] \dfrac{3u-1}{2}, & \dfrac{17}{25}\le u\le1. \end{cases} $$

On the first branch, $p$ is the unique solution of the displayed equation in $[2-3/\sqrt5,\sqrt5/3]$. The function $U$ decreases on this interval. Equivalently, the branch is parametrized by $P_1=\tfrac12+U(p)^2/50$ and $P_2=P_1-\tfrac14+\Phi(p)/40$, with

$$ U(p)=-\frac{15\sqrt5\,p^4+20p^3+18\sqrt5\,p^2-28p-9\sqrt5} {3\sqrt5\,p^4+12p^3+18\sqrt5\,p^2-20p+3\sqrt5}, \qquad r(p)=\frac{2p(5p^2+6\sqrt5\,p-11)}{3\sqrt5\,p^2+2p+3\sqrt5}. $$
$$ \Phi(p)=\frac{2(25p^4+10p^2r+10p^2-24\sqrt5\,pr+19r^2+58r+9)} {5(1+p^2+r)^2}, \qquad r=r(p). $$

The three branches follow from $S_1=10u-5$ and $P_2=u-\tfrac14+S_2/40$ in the exact five-qubit sector-length region.

From sector lengths to reduced purities. We use $P_t=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_t^2)$. Permutation symmetry gives $P_1=(1+S_1/N)/2$ and $P_2=(1+2S_1/N+S_2/\binom{N}{2})/4$. For pure states, $P_t=P_{N-t}$ and $P_N=1$.

The plots apply this invertible affine coordinate change to the exact sector-length sets. This transports every attainable point and preserves the curved, nonconvex boundary for $N=5$. Click inside an attained region, or drag the selected point, to recover a state via the inverse map.
