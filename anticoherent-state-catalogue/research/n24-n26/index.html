24 qubits: explicit maximum-order state

Use normalized Dicke states and set

\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1131}}{128},\qquad\beta=\frac{581\sqrt{1131}}{928}+i\frac{3289\sqrt{3045}}{4640}

The only nonzero coefficients of |\psi_{24}\rangle=\sum_{r=0}^{24}c_r|D_{24}^{(r)}\rangle are

\begin{aligned}c_0=c_{24}&=\alpha,\\c_4=c_{20}&=\frac{42\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{10626}},\\c_8=c_{16}&=\frac{591\alpha-4\beta}{\sqrt{735471}},\\c_{12}&=\frac{2828\alpha+6\beta}{\sqrt{2704156}}.\end{aligned}

They are generated compactly by two octahedral binary forms:

\sum_r\sqrt{\binom{24}{r}}c_rz^r=\alpha(1+14z^4+z^8)^3+\beta z^4(1-z^4)^4.

Exact Schmidt reductions and an independent direct partial trace verify normalization and

\rho_7=I_8/8,\qquad(\rho_8)_{00}-1/9=\frac{13}{155040}\ne0.

Thus this state is exactly seventh-order AC. Its 24 Majorana stars are distinct, verified by an exact polynomial gcd. The state has proper octahedral symmetry.

Why order eight is impossible

Choose the 24 Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal tensor maps of ranks one through four from spin 12 to spin 11. Their pairwise products contain ranks at most eight. An eighth-order AC state would therefore map to 24 nonzero mutually orthogonal vectors in the spin-11 space, which has dimension 23. This contradiction applies to every complex symmetric state.

\langle T_a\psi,T_b\psi\rangle=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(T_a^\dagger T_b)}{25}=\frac{\delta_{ab}}{25},\qquad24>23.

26 qubits: a certified exact algebraic state

The refined Liège state supplies a numerical starting point. A rational contraction certificate proves that an exact state exists nearby. Its definition is

|\psi_{26}\rangle=\sum_{r=0}^{26}\frac{\sqrt{\binom{26}{r}}}{d_r}(x_r^*+ix_{27+r}^*)|D_{26}^{(r)}\rangle.

The certificate records the integer scales d_r, a rational center x^0\in\mathbb Q^{54}, five fixed coordinates, and the order of 49 active coordinates. The exact values x^* are the unique real root in the specified box of the 49 rational quadratic equations equivalent to \rho_6=I_7/7. No closed-form radical components are claimed.

Existence from an exact contraction

After fixing the five rational coordinates, define T(y)=y-AF(y) using the saved rational matrix A. An independent standard-library verifier reconstructs the equations and the exact Jacobian, then proves

\begin{gathered}r=10^{-65},\quad\|AF(y^0)\|_\infty<10^{-67},\\\|I-ADF(y^0)\|_\infty<10^{-76},\quad\|A\|_\infty<17000,\\H\le2^{22},\qquad q\le\|I-ADF(y^0)\|_\infty+\|A\|_\infty Hr<10^{-54}.\end{gathered}

The exact bounds show that T is a contraction and maps the radius-r box strictly into itself. Banach’s theorem gives a unique fixed point. The inverse-defect bound makes A invertible, so this point solves F=0. Its nonsingular rational polynomial equations make its coordinates algebraic. The identity reduction also proves exact normalization.

Each real or imaginary Dicke component is within 2\cdot10^{-65} of the exact rational-center conversion. The saved decimal components and copied nearby Majorana coordinates are numerical renderings; the equations and isolating box define the exact state.

Why order seven is impossible

An exact three-copy positivity certificate excludes every seventh-order pure symmetric state at N=26. The proof uses necessary low-multipole contractions and positive partial time reversal of a rotationally averaged tensor-power projector. A rational separating identity gives a contradiction for every hypothetical state. An independent verifier reconstructs the representation, contractions, partial-transpose embedding and positive-matrix factorizations. This upper bound and the certified sixth-order state prove the maximum six.

21 qubits: what remains open

The exact fourth-order state and universal sixth-order exclusion establish 4\le t_{\max}(21)\le5. The remaining question is whether a fifth-order pure state exists. A failed numerical search or a feasible moment relaxation does not settle that question. The catalogue keeps it unresolved.
