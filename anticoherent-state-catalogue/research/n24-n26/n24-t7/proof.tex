\title{Twenty-four symmetric qubits: maximum pure anticoherence order seven}
\author{Exact analytical construction and tensor-dimension bound}
\date{5 November 2026}\maketitle
\begin{theorem}
The largest anticoherence order of a pure symmetric twenty-four-qubit state is
\[\boxed{t_{\max}(24)=7.}\]
An explicit attaining state is given below in normalized Dicke coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1131}}{128},\qquad
\beta=\frac{581\sqrt{1131}}{928}
+i\frac{3289\sqrt{3045}}{4640}.
\]
Define the seven nonzero Dicke coefficients by
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_0=c_{24}&=\alpha,\\
c_4=c_{20}&=\frac{42\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{10626}},\\
c_8=c_{16}&=\frac{591\alpha-4\beta}{\sqrt{735471}},\\
c_{12}&=\frac{2828\alpha+6\beta}{\sqrt{2704156}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then $|\psi\rangle=\sum_{r=0}^{24}c_r|D_{24}^{(r)}\rangle$ is the promised state.
Equivalently, its coefficient-generating polynomial is
\[
\sum_r\sqrt{\binom{24}{r}}c_r z^r
=\alpha(1+14z^4+z^8)^3+\beta z^4(1-z^4)^4.
\]
These are two octahedral binary forms. Their mixture provides a compact
way to construct the state, but the following verification does not assume
any constellation symmetry.

\paragraph{Exact attainment.}
For normalized Dicke coordinates the $t$-qubit reduction is $\rho_t=M_tM_t^\dagger$,
where
\[
(M_t)_{ab}=c_{a+b}
\sqrt{\frac{\binom ta\binom{24-t}b}{\binom{24}{a+b}}},\qquad
0\le a\le t,\quad0\le b\le24-t.
\]
Substitution of the seven displayed coefficients gives, in exact radical arithmetic,
\[
\sum_r|c_r|^2=1,\qquad \rho_7=\frac{I_8}{8},\qquad
(\rho_8)_{00}-\frac19=\frac{13}{155040}\ne0.
\]
Thus the state has exactly order seven. The independently checked squared
Frobenius defect at the next order is
\[
\left\|\rho_8-\frac{I_9}{9}\right\|_F^2=\frac{2197}{12519480}.
\]
The construction can be recovered using only rational linear equations:
if $f_r$ and $g_r$ are the coefficients of the two polynomial forms divided by
$\sqrt{\binom{24}{r}}$, write
$|c_r|^2=p f_r^2+q g_r^2+2h f_rg_r$.
Normalization and matching the uniform spin moments of orders two, four and six give
\[
p=\frac{1131}{16384},\qquad q=\frac{631449}{320},\qquad
h=\frac{22659}{4096}.
\]
Their determinant $pq-h^2=8859549699/83886080$ is positive.
Taking $\alpha=\sqrt p$ and
$\beta=h/\sqrt p+i\sqrt{q-h^2/p}$ yields the coefficients above.
The portable construction and independent direct partial-trace verifier
check every reduction entry through order eight, without numerical tolerances.

\paragraph{Universal exclusion of order eight.}
Let $\mathcal H_j$ be the spin-$j$ representation and suppose an eighth-order
AC unit vector $\phi\in\mathcal H_{12}$ existed.
Under rotations,
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal H_{12},\mathcal H_{11})
\cong\bigoplus_{L=1}^{23}\mathcal H_L.
\]
Choose Hilbert--Schmidt orthonormal irreducible tensor maps
$T_{Lm}:\mathcal H_{12}\to\mathcal H_{11}$ for $1\le L\le4$.
There are $3+5+7+9=24$ such maps.
Each product $T_{Lm}^\dagger T_{L'm'}$ contains only spin-tensor ranks at most
$L+L'\le8$. Eighth-order anticoherence therefore implies
\[
\langle T_{Lm}\phi,T_{L'm'}\phi\rangle
=\langle\phi|T_{Lm}^\dagger T_{L'm'}|\phi\rangle
=\frac{\Tr(T_{Lm}^\dagger T_{L'm'})}{25}
=\frac{\delta_{LL'}\delta_{mm'}}{25}.
\]
These would be 24 nonzero orthogonal vectors in $\mathcal H_{11}$, whose
dimension is 23, a contradiction. Thus order eight is impossible for every
pure symmetric twenty-four-qubit state. Nesting excludes every higher order;
the explicit seventh-order state proves the maximum.
\end{proof}
