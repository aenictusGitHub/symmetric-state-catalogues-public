21 qubits: maximum order 4

|\psi_{21}\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{915}\bigl(|D_{21}^{(0)}\rangle+|D_{21}^{(21)}\rangle\bigr)+13\sqrt{14}\bigl(|D_{21}^{(4)}\rangle-|D_{21}^{(17)}\rangle\bigr)+17\sqrt7\bigl(|D_{21}^{(8)}\rangle+|D_{21}^{(13)}\rangle\bigr)}{4\sqrt{663}}

All Dicke states are normalized. Exact partial traces give \rho_q=I_{q+1}/(q+1)\quad(1\le q\le 4). The next reduction is not scalar; for example,

(\rho_{5}-I_{6}/6)_{0,5}=\frac{11}{912}\ne0

A hypothetical fifth-order state would give a positive, trace-one rotationally averaged six-copy moment whose low-multipole contractions vanish. A positive combination of these equations yields a contradiction. The complete symmetric representation and the certificate positivity are verified with outward-rounded interval arithmetic, including rigorous representation-error bounds. The verifier uses direct factorial Clebsch–Gordan contractions; floating eigenvalues are not used as proof bounds.

This closes fifth-order existence at N=21: no such pure state exists, and the displayed fourth-order state is globally optimal.

A hypothetical sixth-order state gives a positive, trace-one four-copy moment obeying the vanishing-multipole contraction equations. An exact rational separating identity expresses its negative trace as a sum of the vanishing equations and nonnegative matrix traces. Independent exact reconstruction gives a contradiction, proving maximum order five.

The state also spans an optimal fifth-order AC line: K(22,5)=1. The general rectangular-tensor bound gives K(22,5)\le\lfloor22/12\rfloor=1.

Consequences and auxiliary certificates

Every higher AC order is excluded by nesting. Since every nonzero vector of an AC subspace is an AC state, the pure-state exclusions also give K(21,t)=0\ (t\ge5),\qquad K(22,t)=0\ (t\ge6).

The earlier three-copy sixth-order exclusion at N=21 and three-copy seventh-order exclusion at N=22 are retained as auxiliary proofs. The stronger next-order exclusions above establish the maxima.
