27 qubits: order eight is impossible

No pure symmetric 27-qubit state is eighth-order anticoherent. Consequently, the maximum pure AC order is at most seven.

tmax(27) ≤ 7; K(27,t) = 0 for t ≥ 8.

This result does not settle whether sixth- or seventh-order states exist. It does not prove that the reported fifth-order value is optimal, or certify an exact fifth-order state.

The proof

A hypothetical eighth-order state would give a positive, trace-one, rotation-invariant three-copy moment on the 4,060-dimensional symmetric tensor space. Its spin multipoles through rank eight vanish, forcing necessary linear contraction equations on this moment.

The supplied rational certificate expresses minus its trace as a linear combination of those zero contractions plus positive matrix terms:

−Tr X = Σa ya Ra(B) + ΣJ Tr(CJ xJ), with CJ positive definite and xJ positive semidefinite.

The hypothetical state would make the left side equal to −1 and the right side nonnegative. This contradiction excludes every complex pure symmetric state, without assuming a constellation symmetry.

Reproduce the result offline

Save the certificate and verifier in the same folder, then run:

python3 verify_independently.py

The verifier uses only Python’s standard library. It reconstructs the physical representation and contractions, checks the complete rational separating identity, and proves positivity by exact matrix factorizations. No numerical solver is needed.
